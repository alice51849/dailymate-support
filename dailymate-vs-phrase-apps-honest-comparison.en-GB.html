DailyMate vs Declan Phrases vs Duolingo vs Babbel: an honest comparison for learning everyday phrases

Last checked: 26 November 2026. App Store details change; tap through to each listing for the current version and price.

We make DailyMate. This page compares it with three other apps for learning a language or everyday phrases, using only what each app’s own UK App Store listing says. Where another app does better, we say so.

Quick answer

  • Want full courses with grammar, writing and guided conversation practice? Duolingo or Babbel are built for that. DailyMate is not a full course.
  • Want a phrase app with spaced-repetition review, slower audio and grammar notes, and you only need one or two languages? Declan Phrases (listed on the App Store as “Phrases: Speak a New Language” by Declan Software) is strong here, and you can use three topics in every course before paying.
  • Want complete everyday sentences and natural replies in many languages for one price, with a Home Screen widget and Apple Watch? That is what DailyMate is built for.

Where the others are stronger

  • Duolingo and Babbel teach a language as a course: grammar, writing and structured lessons, with a huge base of ratings. If your goal is to learn a language properly over months, either may serve you better than a phrase app.
  • Babbel also lets you download lessons for offline use and has a review system; DailyMate has no review system.
  • Declan Phrases offers spaced-repetition review, slower audio, up to seven ways to practise each phrase, grammar breakdowns (20 languages so far) and offline use. You can use three topics in each course for free; DailyMate has no free part.
  • DailyMate does not have enough UK ratings to show yet, so you have less to go on from other users.

Where DailyMate is different

  • One purchase, every language. The one upfront price includes the full app in all 47 learning languages on iPhone, iPad, the interactive widget and Apple Watch. No subscription and nothing extra to buy.
  • Phrases with their replies. It teaches complete sentences and the natural replies that follow, organised by 84 real-life situations, from greetings, food and shopping to work, health and travel.
  • Three clear levels. Each topic has its own 200 beginner, 200 intermediate and 200 advanced phrases.
  • Practice outside the app. The Home Screen widget lets you view a phrase, move on or save it; Apple Watch keeps phrases, listening, favourites and progress on your wrist.
  • Separate progress per language. Favourites and progress are kept separately for each language you learn.

Prefer to start with a free app?

DailyMate Lite is a separate free app from the same developer, built around cue-and-reply pairs in 50 learning languages. You can practise the complete Travel topic at Beginner level free; other topics and levels unlock with a one-time Lifetime Unlock (price shown on the App Store).
