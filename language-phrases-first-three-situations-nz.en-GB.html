Language phrases: start with three situations, not a whole phrasebook

A full phrasebook is hard to use in the moment. If you are learning language phrases for a trip, three situations cover a lot of the first conversations: greeting someone, ordering food and travel.

The first three situations

SituationWhy it comes first
GreetingsMost exchanges open here, and each greeting has a natural reply.
Food and diningOrdering is one of the first things many travellers do, and each phrase comes with the reply.
TravelAsking for directions or transport is common on a trip, and each phrase comes with the reply.

Learn each phrase with its reply

DailyMate organises 8,400 practical phrases into 14 everyday topics and 84 real-life situations in 47 learning languages. Each phrase comes with the natural reply that follows, so the three situations become three short exchanges rather than three lists.

Stay at beginner level first

Every topic has 200 beginner, 200 intermediate and 200 advanced phrases. Start at beginner level, then move up to intermediate and advanced when you are ready.

When you are learning more than one language

Favourites and progress are kept separately for each language, so the same three situations can be learned in two languages without mixing them up. More on that in several languages, no mix-ups.
